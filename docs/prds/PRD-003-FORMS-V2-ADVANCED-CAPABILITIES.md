## 1. Business Problem

Forms V1 deliberately shipped with a single, flat form concept — deferred capabilities included OTP verification, multi-step forms, and lead scoring/qualification (`PRD-001` §7 Out of Scope; §34 Future Considerations explicitly names these as candidates "if justified by a concrete use case"). The legacy screenshots are evidence that real business demand for these capabilities existed and was previously monetized as tiered form products.

That justification now exists: tenants want more sophisticated lead-capture behavior than V1's single-step form supports, and Innovoot now has (via `PRD-002`) a real mechanism to gate and monetize such capabilities without Forms itself owning commercial logic. Without this PRD, that demand has no path into the product — V1 cannot absorb it without violating its own confirmed "no typed variants" boundary, and there is no defined way for a paid capability to attach to a Form.

## 2. Objective

Add four capabilities on top of the unchanged V1 Form/Submission/Lead model, opt-in per Form and checked through Billing's entitlement contract rather than any Forms-owned commercial logic — but these four are not equivalent in kind:

- **Three independently entitlement-gated Form capabilities:** OTP Verification, Multi-step Forms, and AI Lead Qualification. Each is opt-in per Form, each is checked against its own entitlement key, and each is safely reversible (per `PRD-002` §11.1's downgrade/fallback contract).
- **One Forms-facing commercial/navigation experience:** Tier / Plan Selection / Plan & Capability visibility. This is **not** described as an entitlement and is **not** independently entitlement-gated (`forms.tier_selection_ui` is not created, per §10.4) — it is a display/navigation surface into Billing-owned Plan management, not a capability a tenant is entitled to or locked out of.

**Explicitly not the objective:** reintroducing legacy form-type variants (Starter/Verified/Smart/Conversion Pro as distinct products), reproducing the legacy pricing/comparison table as commercial truth, or giving Forms V2 any pricing, payment, subscription, or entitlement logic of its own.

## 4. Scope

### In Scope

| Capability               | What's added                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| OTP Verification         | An optional per-Form requirement that a visitor's mobile number be OTP-verified before a Submission is accepted                                                                                                                                                  |
| Multi-step Forms         | An optional per-Form presentation mode that groups the Form's fields across sequential steps instead of one view                                                                                                                                                 |
| AI Lead Qualification    | An optional per-Form capability that runs some form of qualification in connection with a Submission — **scope limited to the capability slot and its entitlement gating/fallback contract; the actual qualification logic/criteria is OPEN, see §10.3 and §15** |
| Tier / Plan Selection UI | The Forms-facing entry point that shows a tenant's current Plan, which capabilities are included/unavailable, and a path to Billing's Plan Overview/Comparison/Change flow — **not a Forms-owned Plan system; see §10.4**                                        |

All four are additive to V1 (never replacing V1's field types, Submission model, or Lead creation) and require a safe fallback so a Form never breaks. **They are not four equivalent paid entitlements, however:**

- **OTP Verification, Multi-step Forms, and AI Lead Qualification are Form capabilities** — each independently gated by its own entitlement key (`forms.otp_verification`, `forms.multi_step`, `forms.ai_lead_qualification`).
- **Tier / Plan Selection UI is a Forms-facing commercial/navigation surface that integrates with Billing** — it is not, by default, an independent gated capability, and does not get its own entitlement key unless a separate product decision establishes that the UI itself should be gated (see §10.4).

### 6.2 Billing & Entitlements Dependency (restated, not redefined)

Forms V2 consumes — and never redefines — the following from `PRD-002`:

- **Model:** `Plan + Subscription State → Entitlement Service → Capability` (`PRD-002` §10, decision #4)
- **Entitlement keys Forms V2 checks:**
  - `forms.otp_verification`
  - `forms.multi_step`
  - `forms.ai_lead_qualification`
- **Forms V2 never checks:** Plan name, Plan price, ₹200/₹500/₹999 or any other price figure, payment gateway, or subscription amount. This is a hard constraint (decision #9, §14) — any implementation that branches on a Plan name or price instead of an entitlement key is a defect against this PRD.
- **States Forms V2 must handle, per capability** (inherited verbatim from `PRD-002` §10.1, not redefined here): Active / Not available / Subscription inactive / Downgrade-while-in-use / Entitlement-check failure.
- **Fail-closed rule** (`PRD-002` §10.1 State E, §15.1): if the entitlement check fails, Forms V2 must treat the specific capability as not-entitled — never fail open. Per `PRD-002` §11.1's governing principle ("a downgrade removes the paid capability, not the entire published Form"), a failed check disables the _capability_, not the whole Form.
- **Enforcement principle** (`PRD-002` §10.1, §15.1): re-validated at Admin configuration, widget render, _and_ public submission-time — not only when the Form was last edited.
- **Visible-but-locked** (`PRD-002` §10.2, §12.1): every gated capability in the Form editor stays visible when not entitled, with a reason and a link to Billing's Plan Overview — never hidden.
- **Downgrade fallback per capability** (`PRD-002` §11.1 table, reused verbatim in §10 below).

## 8. User Journeys

### Journey A — Tenant Admin enables an entitled capability

1. Tenant Admin opens a Form's editor (existing V1 screen).
2. The capability toggle panel (§10.4) shows OTP, multi-step, and AI qualification, each reflecting current entitlement state.
3. For a capability the tenant is entitled to (Active state), the toggle is enabled; turning it on reveals its configuration (OTP toggle, or the step-builder, or the qualification panel).
4. Saving the Form persists the capability configuration alongside the existing V1 field/CTA definition — one Form, one save action.

### Journey B — Tenant Admin encounters a locked capability

1. Same entry point as Journey A.
2. A capability the tenant is _not_ entitled to (Not-available or Subscription-inactive state) appears visible but locked, with a reason and a link to Billing's Plan Overview (`PRD-002` §9) — Forms V2 does not render its own pricing/plan UI, it links out to Billing's.
3. Tenant Admin follows the link to request a plan change (per `PRD-002`'s confirmed OPS-assignment lifecycle) — that flow is entirely owned by Billing, not restated here.

### Journey C — Visitor submits an OTP-gated Form

1. Visitor fills the Form (single-step or as part of a multi-step flow) on the widget or hosted page.
2. If OTP verification is active for this Form, the visitor is prompted to verify their mobile number before final submission.
3. On successful verification, the Submission proceeds through V1's existing validation and Lead-creation behavior, unchanged.
4. If OTP verification cannot be enforced (entitlement check failure or lapsed subscription), the Form falls back to normal submission without a verification step (per `PRD-002` §11.1 fallback table) — the visitor is never blocked by a billing-side problem they have no way to resolve.

### Journey D — Visitor completes a multi-step Form

1. Visitor sees Step 1 of the Form; per-step validation applies before advancing (evidence: legacy `SYSTEM_FLOW.md` — confirmed present in project knowledge, explicitly categorized as legacy evidence, not a requirement source, by `FORMS-V1-API-BEHAVIORAL-REQUIREMENTS.md`'s own source list: _"Legacy Admin and InnoForms documentation (SYSTEM_FLOW.md, PROJECT.md, SHARED_RUNTIME_OWNERSHIP.md, etc.) is evidence only and does not define any requirement."_ Used here strictly as interaction-pattern evidence, not carried forward as a fixed rule).
2. Visitor can move forward/back between steps without losing entered data.
3. On the final step, the visitor submits — this produces exactly one Submission and one Lead, identical in structure to a V1 single-step Form's submission (§6.1).
4. If multi-step cannot be enforced, the Form falls back to a single-step presentation of the same field set (per `PRD-002` §11.1) — the visitor sees one form, not a broken partial flow.

### Journey E — Entitlement lapses while a capability is in active, public use

1. A tenant's Plan changes (via Billing's OPS-assignment lifecycle, `PRD-002` §14.6) such that a capability the tenant was using is no longer entitled.
2. Per `PRD-002` §11.1/§15.5, the affected capability is immediately disabled on the live Form, which falls back to its defined safe behavior (§10 below) — the Form itself is never taken down.
3. Per `PRD-002` §12.2, the tenant sees a proactive notice in Admin identifying what was disabled and on which Form(s).

### Journey F — AI Lead Qualification runs after submission

1. A visitor submits a Form with AI Lead Qualification active — the experience is identical to a normal V1 submission; no additional visitor-facing questions or steps.
2. The Submission is validated and captured per V1; a Lead is created per V1's unconditional Submission→Lead rule. AI qualification is never a gate on this step.
3. AI qualification runs against the submitted Lead/Form data, producing a Qualification Category (e.g. Qualified / Needs Review / Low Priority) and a short Explanation, attached to the Lead. No numeric score or confidence indicator is produced at any point.
4. **Scenario A — not entitled:** AI capability unavailable; Lead is captured normally, without AI qualification (`PRD-002` §11.1 fallback).
5. **Scenario B — entitled, but the AI service itself fails/times out:** Lead is still captured normally; the qualification result is simply unavailable for that Lead — this must never block Submission or Lead creation.
6. Tenant Admin, reviewing Leads, can see the Category and Explanation where they apply — labeled clearly as AI-generated. **Exact placement (Lead Detail vs. Leads List vs. elsewhere) is a non-blocking UI-presentation detail, deferred to UX Spec stage — the conflict over whether this is allowed at all is resolved (§10.3).**

### Notes on the two rows that aren't simple gaps

**Lead integration — this may conflict with an existing approved V1 decision, not just be undefined.** `FORM_UX_SPECIFICATION_V1.md` §12 states Lead Detail explicitly **excludes** "scoring" from its confirmed scope: _"Explicitly excluded, per instruction and CONFIRMED scope: assignment, ownership, pipeline, scoring, tagging, notes, bulk actions, export."_ If AI Lead Qualification's output is a score (as legacy evidence suggests, though not confirmed for V2), displaying it on the existing V1 Lead Detail screen would sit in tension with that confirmed exclusion — unless "scoring" there specifically meant manual/CRM-style lead scoring (a human or pipeline concept) rather than an automated qualification attribute, which is a real possible distinction but not one that's been drawn anywhere. This needs your call, not an inference on my part, and directly affects whether qualification output needs its own new surface or can extend V1's existing Lead Detail.

**Failure behavior — only partially confirmed.** `PRD-002` §11.1's fallback covers the case where the _entitlement_ isn't active (not entitled, lapsed, or check failed) — "qualification stops running, Lead still captured." What's still open is a narrower case: the tenant _is_ entitled and the capability _is_ active, but the AI service itself fails at runtime (timeout, provider outage). Whether that should behave identically to the entitlement-fallback case (Lead captured without qualification) or differently (e.g., retry, queue for later) is not decided.

**Data/privacy — flagged, not just open.** Sending submitted Lead data to any AI evaluation process is a new privacy surface not addressed by `DATA_PRIVACY_PRINCIPLES.md` as it stands — that document governs ordinary contact/lead data handling in general, but doesn't address routing data through a processing step that behaves like a sub-processor. Depending on how this is built, it may need to be checked against `DATA_PRIVACY_PRINCIPLES.md` §14 (sub-processor tracking) once defined — flagged for awareness, not resolved here.
